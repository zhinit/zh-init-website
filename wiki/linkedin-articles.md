# LinkedIn Articles

How to write and publish long-form articles on LinkedIn. For format comparisons,
see [[linkedin-content-types]]. For programmatic publishing, see
[[linkedin-api]].

## Publishing Workflow

1. Go to your LinkedIn homepage and click "Write article" at the top of the
   feed.
2. Enter a headline. Specific, opinionated titles outperform vague ones.
3. Add a cover image. Optimal dimensions: 1200 × 627 pixels.
4. Write and format using the editor (see formatting options below).
5. Embed rich media as needed (videos, presentations, images).
6. Publish.
7. Post your most important link as the first comment (not in the article body)
   to avoid the ~20–35% reach penalty from outbound links.

(source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md)

## Formatting Options

The LinkedIn article editor provides:

- **Headings** — H1 and H2
- **Text styling** — bold, italic
- **Lists** — numbered and bulleted
- **Blockquotes** — for callouts or quotes
- **Rich media** — embedded videos, SlideShare presentations, image galleries

(source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md)

Notable absences: no code blocks, no syntax highlighting, no markdown support,
no custom HTML, no footnotes. The editor is closer to Medium than to a technical
blogging platform.

## Content Specifications

- Maximum length: 125,000 characters (source: linkedin-help-content-types.md)
- Recommended length: 1,000–2,000 words for optimal engagement (source:
  evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md)
- Cover image: 1200 × 627 px (source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md)

## SEO and Discoverability

Articles are published at `linkedin.com/pulse/` URLs and indexed by Google.
LinkedIn's domain authority is high, so well-structured articles can surface in
Google results and AI Overviews faster than posts on newer personal blogs
(source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).

Articles also receive permanent placement on your profile under Articles &
Activity, unlike posts which scroll off feeds (source:
evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).

## Distribution Tips

- Use a mix of broad hashtags (#Leadership, #SoftwareEngineering) and niche tags
  (#SaaSGrowth, #SystemDesign) (source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).
- Best posting times: Tuesday–Thursday, 10–11 a.m. for professional audiences
  (source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).
- Avoid embedding outbound links in the article body — place them in the first
  comment instead (source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).
- Lead with a strong hook that addresses the reader's problem (source:
  evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).
- Articles can be edited after publication to maintain relevance (source:
  evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).

## Republishing Blog Posts as Articles

The strongest use case for LinkedIn articles is distribution of existing
content, not original creation. Adapting an existing blog post for LinkedIn
avoids duplicate effort while reaching LinkedIn's professional audience (source:
evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).

Considerations when republishing:

- **Duplicate content** — Google generally handles near-duplicate content across
  domains without penalty, but the version on the higher-authority domain
  (linkedin.com) may outrank your personal blog in search results.
- **Canonical URLs** — LinkedIn's article editor does not support `<link
  rel="canonical">` tags. You cannot point Google back to your original post.
- **Alternatives** — instead of full republication, post a summary or excerpt as
  a LinkedIn post with a link to the full piece on your site (placed in the
  first comment to avoid reach penalty).

## Articles vs. Newsletters

Both use the same rich-text editor and the same `linkedin.com/pulse/` URL
structure. The differences:

| Aspect        | Article                    | Newsletter                        |
|---------------|----------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Subscriber model | None                    | Email + in-app notifications      |
| Availability  | All members                | Requires Creator Mode             |
| Cadence       | Ad hoc                     | Regular/recurring                 |
| SEO           | Identical                  | Identical                         |

(source: brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md)

Use articles for one-off deep dives. Use newsletters for recurring series where
building a subscriber base matters.
