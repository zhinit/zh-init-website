# Color and Cognition

How color affects intelligence, creativity, memory, and learning performance.

## Effects by color

### Red impairs analytical performance

Viewing red prior to a challenging cognitive task undermines performance. Red
increases avoidance motivation in achievement contexts — the association with
danger/failure triggers caution that interferes with complex thinking (source:
elliot-2015-color-psychological-functioning-review.md).

### Blue enhances alertness and problem-solving

Blue activates the melanopsin photoreceptor system, increasing subjective
alertness and attention-task performance. Blue environments lower stress while
enhancing problem-solving abilities (source:
elliot-2015-color-psychological-functioning-review.md,
imotions-color-human-behavior.md).

### Green enhances creativity

Four experiments (~200 adults) showed that a brief flash of green before a
creativity task produced more creative ideas than white, grey, red, or blue.
The proposed mechanism: green signals growth — physical (plants) and
psychological — priming mastery motivation. "Green may serve as a cue that
evokes the motivation to strive for improvement and task mastery, which in turn
may facilitate growth" (source: lichtenfeld-2012-fertile-green-creativity.md).

Even short exposure to green improves working memory and restores mental energy
after intense cognitive tasks (source: cognifit-colors-that-calm-the-mind.md).

## Color and memory

Color enhances recognition memory by approximately 5–10% compared to
grayscale. Two independent mechanisms:

1. **Attention capture:** Color draws the eye more effectively than other
   visual features. Warm colors (red, orange, yellow) have stronger
   attention-capturing effects than cool colors.
2. **Emotional arousal:** Colors trigger emotional responses that enhance
   memory consolidation, independent of the attentional pathway.

Critical factors for effectiveness:
- Encoding-retrieval consistency — colors during learning should match testing
- High contrast combinations (white on black, white on red) outperform low contrast
- Color must be spatially integrated with the information, not separated

(source: dzulkifli-mustafar-2013-colour-memory-performance.md)

## Green environments and learning

A within-subjects study (N=65 children, ages 7–9) compared identical
45-minute lessons taught in a green school garden vs. a classroom. After
garden lessons, children showed:

- Significantly better selective attention (Bells Test)
- Higher math calculation performance on both simple and complex problems
- Increased positive affect (for emotionally vulnerable students)
- Higher perceived environmental restorativeness

The effect was strongest for students with self-reported emotional
difficulties, suggesting green environments particularly support learners
who need calming (source: berto-2022-green-school-environment-cognition.md).

## Practical implications

- Blue and green in learning environments enhance concentration and
  problem-solving (source: imotions-color-human-behavior.md)
- Green exposure before or during creative work may prime creative output
- Color-coded educational materials can improve retention, but the specific
  colors and contrast ratios matter
- The saturation caveat applies: muted blues and greens support focus;
  saturated versions may energize instead — see [[color-psychology]]
