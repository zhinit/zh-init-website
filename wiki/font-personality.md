# Font Personality

Typefaces carry personality traits independent of the words they display. This
page maps font categories and specific typefaces to their perceived traits,
based on experimental research.

## The three-factor model

Shaikh & Chaparro's factor analysis of 40 typefaces (N=379) identified three
dimensions of typeface personality (source: designmodo-font-psychology.md):

| Factor | Measures | High scorers |
|--------|----------|--------------|
| Potency | Ruggedness, masculinity, strength | Bold display typefaces |
| Evaluative | Perceived beauty, value, elegance | Script typefaces, display typefaces |
| Activity | Excitement, loudness, speed | Display typefaces |

Serif and sans-serif typefaces were neutral across all three factors — they are
the baseline. Script fonts scored low on Potency but high on Evaluative (beautiful
but not strong). Bold display fonts scored high on both Potency and Evaluative.

## Font weight and style effects

- **Bold** conveys strength, assertiveness, authority (source:
  brandvision-typography-influence-perception.md)
- **Light weights** suggest subtlety and elegance (source:
  designmodo-font-psychology.md)
- **Italic** in serif fonts increases eye fixation time; problematic for
  dyslexic readers (source: cognitiontoday-font-psychology-research.md)

## Context dependence

The IBM study demonstrated that the "best" font varies by context: Baskerville
was most trustworthy for banking and news sites, but Fira Sans outperformed it
on a fitness site. Helvetica led in trustworthiness, usability, and appeal for
a clothing site (source: ibm-design-fonts-influence-perception-2016.md).

Brand examples reinforce this: Netflix uses sans-serif for modern shows,
script for creative content, and serif for historical programming (source:
designmodo-font-psychology.md).
