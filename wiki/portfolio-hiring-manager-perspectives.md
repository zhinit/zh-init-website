# Portfolio: Hiring Manager Perspectives

Synthesized views from three primary sources: a survey of 60+ hiring managers
(source: kettmann-dont-waste-time-portfolio-website.md), interviews with 6
named hiring managers (source: muse-6-hiring-managers-personal-websites.md),
and ~50 practitioner accounts including several self-identified hiring managers
(source: hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md).

## What hiring managers look at

### The bio

The first thing most hiring managers focus on is who you are and what you do.
"A great two- to five-line bio that sums up who you are and what your value
proposition is" — Deniz Gültekin, Eventbrite. "That's probably the first item
the hiring manager will focus on" — Kelli Dragovich, Hired (source:
muse-6-hiring-managers-personal-websites.md).

### How you think

Multiple HMs said showing your thought process matters more than showing
polished output. Ed Fry (Inbound.org) looks for "a list of projects, along
with explanations as to what those projects were about" including objectives,
decision-making approaches, and outcomes. Joel Klettke (Business Casual
Copywriting) said showing how candidates think is "so key, but so overlooked"
(source: muse-6-hiring-managers-personal-websites.md).

Even failed projects are valuable when you explain what you learned (source:
muse-6-hiring-managers-personal-websites.md, Ed Fry).

### Code quality and working demos

85% of hiring managers value problem-solving skills above all else. 84% want
working applications, not just repositories (source:
hakia-developer-portfolio-guide-2026.md). Clean, readable code with proper
structure is a critical factor (source: hakia-developer-portfolio-guide-2026.md).

### Relevance

Content should align with the job being sought. Ty Magnin (Appcues) warned
that unrelated focuses may signal divided commitment (source:
muse-6-hiring-managers-personal-websites.md).

## What hiring managers don't care about

### Visual design (mostly)

Design doesn't need to be gorgeous, but it can't look neglected. Poor design
"suggests being out of touch" — Ty Magnin. It's a floor, not a ceiling
(source: muse-6-hiring-managers-personal-websites.md).

One exception: bschmidt1 (HN, self-identified hiring manager) said he doesn't
interview frontend developers or designers without personal sites, and noted
that a simple white grid layout got more compliments than flashy Three.js
experiments (source: hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md).

### Quantity of projects

3-5 polished projects beat 10+ basic ones. No source recommends maximizing
project count (source: hakia-developer-portfolio-guide-2026.md,
nucamp-portfolio-projects-recruiters.md).

### The portfolio website itself (as distinct from its content)

51% of surveyed hiring managers said lacking a portfolio website would not
reduce a candidate's chances (source:
kettmann-dont-waste-time-portfolio-website.md). The website is a delivery
mechanism. What's delivered — projects, writing, evidence of thinking — is
what matters.

## The blog advantage

The strongest career outcomes in practitioner accounts came from technical
blogging, not project portfolios. Patterns from the HN thread (source:
hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md):

- **rwieruch**: React blog led to freelance career; clients reached out
  through the blog for 7 years
- **nickjj**: "Nearly every single professional opportunity that has come my
  way was through my blog" over ~9 years
- **phrotoma**: K8s and Istio blog posts led to employer finding and recruiting
  them for a training program
- **woodrowbarlow**: one HN-frontpage post generated a surge of recruiter
  contact within a week
- **giansegato**: site was "higher ROI than anything else I've ever done for
  my career, by a long shot" — investors, visa sponsorship, professional
  network

The pattern: deep technical content on specific topics, written over time,
discoverable via search engines. Not link roundups or "my journey into tech"
posts.

Several hiring managers in the thread confirmed they look at personal websites
when evaluating candidates. flpm: "When I am hiring I always look at personal
websites if they are available." ratedgene: awards points for communication
skills and critical thinking when candidates maintain sites (source:
hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md).

## Negative signals

A portfolio can hurt when:

- **Design is poor** — "a bad design can make you look incompetent even though
  everything works fine" (source: kettmann-dont-waste-time-portfolio-website.md)
- **Content is outdated** — flpm counts poor-quality sites with outdated
  content against candidates (source: hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md)
- **Personal opinions misalign** — lnsru warned that blog opinions can
  conflict with employer values (source:
  hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md)
- **It's generic** — lacking personalization raises concerns about creativity
  (source: muse-6-hiring-managers-personal-websites.md, Brian Sparker)

## Sources disagree on

**Whether a portfolio website is necessary at all.** The Kettmann survey says
no (51% of HMs say no difference). The Nucamp article says 87% of recruiters
consider portfolios crucial. The HN thread is split roughly 60/40 in favor.
The discrepancy may reflect different definitions of "portfolio" (website vs.
body of work) and different hiring contexts (startup vs. enterprise, frontend
vs. backend).
