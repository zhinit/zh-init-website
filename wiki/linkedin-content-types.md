# LinkedIn Content Types

LinkedIn offers three formats for publishing original content: posts, articles,
and newsletters. Each has different reach mechanics, formatting capabilities,
and persistence.

## Posts

Short-form updates in the LinkedIn feed. Limited to 3,000 characters (source:
evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md). Can include images, videos,
documents, polls, and links. Posts have the highest immediate feed visibility
but are ephemeral — they scroll off feeds quickly and are not permanently
accessible from your profile.

Posts are the default format for sharing links to external content (e.g., a blog
post hosted on your own site).

## Articles

Long-form content published natively on LinkedIn. Up to 125,000 characters
(source: linkedin-help-content-types.md). Use a rich-text editor with headings
(H1, H2), bold, italic, lists, blockquotes, and embedded media (images, videos,
SlideShare presentations) (source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).

Key properties:

- **Permanent profile placement** — articles live under your Articles & Activity
  section indefinitely (source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).
- **Google-indexed** — published at `linkedin.com/pulse/` URLs, benefiting from
  LinkedIn's high domain authority (source:
  brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md).
- **Distribution** — feed (connections + followers) and LinkedIn search. No
  subscriber model (source: brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md).
- **Availability** — all LinkedIn members can publish articles (source:
  brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md).

See [[linkedin-articles]] for the full publishing workflow and formatting
details.

## Newsletters

Recurring publications with the same rich-text editor as articles.
Differentiated by a subscriber model: subscribers receive email notifications
and in-app alerts when a new edition is published (source:
linkedin-help-content-types.md). This is a third distribution channel beyond
feed and search (source: brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md).

Key properties:

- **Requires Creator Mode** on personal profiles, or Page admin access for
  company pages (source: brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md).
- **Subscriber base** — partially independent of your standard LinkedIn network.
  Subscribers opted in intentionally (source:
  brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md).
- **Same SEO** — no clear SEO advantage over articles; both use
  `linkedin.com/pulse/` URLs (source:
  brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md).

## When to Use Each

| Format     | Best for                                      |
|------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Post       | Quick updates, sharing external links, engagement |
| Article    | Standalone deep dives, case studies, SEO-targeted pieces |
| Newsletter | Recurring series, building a subscriber audience |

(source: brandghost-linkedin-articles-vs-newsletters.md)

## Reposts

Sharing content created by other LinkedIn members. Not a publishing format —
included here for completeness (source: linkedin-help-content-types.md).

## Distribution and Outbound Links

Outbound links in articles or posts reduce algorithmic reach by roughly 20–35%.
LinkedIn's algorithm favors content that keeps users on-platform. The common
workaround is to place the link in the first comment instead of the post body
(source: evergreenfeed-linkedin-articles-guide.md).
