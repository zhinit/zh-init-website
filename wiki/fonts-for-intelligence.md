# Fonts for Intelligence

Fonts that convey trustworthiness, authority, credibility, and intellectual
seriousness. The perception of intelligence in typography operates through
cognitive fluency, cultural association, and specific structural features.

## Why some fonts look smarter

### Cognitive fluency

The primary mechanism: clear, familiar fonts make statements feel more true.
Schwarz and Reber demonstrated that high-contrast, easy-to-read typography
increased perceived truthfulness significantly. The brain equates reading ease
with truthfulness (source: cognitiontoday-font-psychology-research.md).

### Cultural conditioning

Serif fonts carry centuries of association with books, academia, newspapers,
and legal documents. This cultural weight is itself a signal — the Monotype
study found that serif typeface Cotford's association with luxury and fashion
was attributed to cultural conditioning (source:
monotype-font-emotion-study-2022.md).

### Structural maturity

Shaikh & Chaparro found serif fonts scored highest on "stable, practical,
mature, formal" — the cluster of traits associated with intellectual authority
(source: designmodo-font-psychology.md).

## Context dependence

The IBM study's most important finding: the "smartest" font depends on context.
Baskerville dominated banking and news but lost to Fira Sans on fitness and to
Helvetica on clothing. Choose based on what your specific audience expects
intelligence to look like (source:
ibm-design-fonts-influence-perception-2016.md).

## What undermines perceived intelligence

- Novelty/display fonts — high Activity factor reads as loud, not smart
- Script fonts — high Evaluative but low Potency; beautiful, not authoritative
- Comic Sans — consistently lowest trustworthiness in experiments
- Poor legibility — cognitive disfluency may aid memory but hurts trust
- Inconsistent hierarchy — undermines the perception of careful thought
