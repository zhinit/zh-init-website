# Portfolio Website

A personal website where a developer presents their work, writing, and
professional identity. For CS students and junior developers seeking jobs, the
portfolio website is one component of a broader professional presence that
includes GitHub, LinkedIn, and a resume.

## Does it matter?

The evidence is mixed but leans toward "yes, with caveats."

In a survey of 60+ hiring managers, 93% said they would look at a portfolio
website if available, but 51% said lacking one would not reduce a candidate's
chances compared to someone who had one (source:
kettmann-dont-waste-time-portfolio-website.md). A portfolio website is
nice-to-have, not a gate.

However, the strongest career outcomes reported in practitioner accounts come
from people who maintained a web presence with substantive content — technical
blog posts, working demos, niche tools (source:
hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md). Multiple developers described their
site as "the highest ROI career investment" they made, though several noted it
takes years to compound (source: hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md,
giansegato comment).

Counterpoint: some developers report maintaining quality sites for years with
no career impact. Selection bias is real — success stories are louder than
non-events (source: hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md, j0hnyl and Maro
comments).

## What the site should be

The sites that generate career outcomes are not brochure-style portfolios with
hero sections and skill bar charts. They fall into two categories:

1. **Project showcases with working demos** — live applications a recruiter
   can open and use, backed by clean GitHub repos. See
   [[portfolio-projects]].

2. **Technical blogs** — posts demonstrating deep thinking on specific topics.
   The blog is more valuable than the portfolio grid. Multiple hiring managers
   said they care most about seeing how a candidate thinks (source:
   muse-6-hiring-managers-personal-websites.md, Ed Fry and Joel Klettke
   quotes). Posts about K8s, multiplayer networking, React patterns, and
   security research generated direct job offers in practitioner accounts
   (source: hn-personal-website-helped-get-hired.md).

See [[portfolio-design]] for visual and structural decisions.

## The 15-second window

Recruiters spend approximately 15 seconds on a first visit (source:
hakia-developer-portfolio-guide-2026.md). This means:

- Identity must be immediate: name, what you do, one line
- Project links must be visible without scrolling
- Working demos must load fast and function
- Everything else is second-visit material

## The contrarian case

The argument against portfolio websites is about opportunity cost, not
existence (source: kettmann-dont-waste-time-portfolio-website.md). Spending 40
hours on custom CSS is low-ROI when the same time could build a project.
Static portfolio sites duplicate information already on LinkedIn and GitHub.
A poorly designed site can actively hurt — "a bad design can make you look
incompetent even though everything works fine" (source:
kettmann-dont-waste-time-portfolio-website.md).

The resolution: have a place on the web where your work lives, but spend
minimal time on the site itself and maximum time on the work it presents. Use
a template or static site generator. The site is a frame, not the art.

## What a portfolio website is not

It is not a substitute for:
- A polished resume (source: kettmann-dont-waste-time-portfolio-website.md)
- A maintained GitHub profile with clean READMEs (source:
  hakia-developer-portfolio-guide-2026.md)
- An active LinkedIn presence (source: sjonany-cs-job-hunt-2026.md)
- Networking — meetups, cold emails, referrals (source:
  sjonany-cs-job-hunt-2026.md)

All four are prerequisites. The website supplements them.
